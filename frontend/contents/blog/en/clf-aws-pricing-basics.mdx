# AWS Pricing Basics for Cloud Practitioner

Pricing is something you cannot skip when learning AWS. The Cloud Practitioner exam asks not only about technical services, but also about billing, pricing, support, and cost management.

## Summary

The core idea of AWS pricing is "pay only for what you use." But each service charges for different things. You need to understand what is billed: storage, requests, data transfer, runtime, and so on.

## Pay-as-you-go

Most AWS services use pay-as-you-go pricing. For example, S3 charges for storage and requests, Lambda charges for execution count and duration, and CloudFront charges for data transfer and requests.

The important thing is to separate services that tend to incur fixed costs just by being created from services that only charge when you use them.

## Free Tier

Check the account creation date and plan before estimating a free deployment. Accounts created before 2025-07-15 remain on the legacy program, whose 12-month offers were time-limited. Eligible new customers receive USD 100 in credits and can earn up to another USD 100. The Free plan lasts up to six months or until credits run out, whichever comes first; it does not charge until you upgrade to Paid. Paid-plan usage beyond applicable credits or outside eligible offers is billed. Always Free allowances still have usage limits.

The FAQ checked on 2026-11-09 gives conflicting credit-expiry descriptions: General Q6 says six months, while Free Plan Q8 says twelve months after account creation following an upgrade. Do not assume either duration applies to every account. Confirm your own expiry in Settings Portal or the Billing Credits page, and verify eligible services before launching this architecture. AWS Budgets notifications help monitor costs; they are not a guarantee that all resources stop at the budget threshold.

AWS offers a Free Tier. However, "Free Tier" does not mean everything is free forever. There are conditions on time period, usage amount, and which services qualify.

For personal projects, instead of relying only on the Free Tier, it is more important to set up AWS Budgets so you get notified about unexpected charges.

## Services that tend to incur fixed costs

Common examples beginners should watch out for are NAT Gateway, RDS, ALB, EC2, and OpenSearch. These can keep running up charges depending on configuration and usage, so you should be careful with them in a personal MVP.

In this project, the MVP avoids EC2, RDS, NAT Gateway, and ALB, and instead uses S3, CloudFront, API Gateway, Lambda, and DynamoDB.

## Services used for cost management

AWS Budgets is for budget alerts, and Cost Explorer is for analyzing costs by service. Budgets tells you "you went over the limit," and Cost Explorer helps you figure out "where the money went."

## Key points for the exam

- AWS pricing is pay-as-you-go by default
- The Free Tier has conditions
- Budgets is used for budget management
- Cost Explorer is used for cost analysis
- Savings Plans and Reserved Instances offer discounts for long-term usage

## Practical notes

Do not leave pricing as an afterthought in your design. As soon as you decide which services to use, check what each one charges for.

For personal projects, the safe approach is to set up Budgets first, avoid services that tend to incur fixed costs, and prefer static delivery and serverless options.
